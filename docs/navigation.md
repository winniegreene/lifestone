# Navigation: teleport panel, map coordinates and golden viewpoints (M1 wave 2, wave-3 fixes)

**Track:** sky, time of day and navigation UI (see also [sky.md](sky.md)); the wave-3 navigation, UX and legal-notices fixes.
**Code:** `src/Lifestone.Client/Navigation/` (engine-agnostic, tested), `src/Lifestone.Client/LegalNotices.cs`, `src/Lifestone.Game/scripts/UI/` (panels, the About panel and the `Navigator` that moves the camera; `NavigationUi` wires them), `src/Lifestone.Game/scripts/Player/` (camera and position HUD), `src/Lifestone.Game/scripts/Diagnostics/{GoldenViewpointsRunner,CaptureReadiness,TeleportTourRunner}.cs`, and the data in `src/Lifestone.Data/World/Data/`.

## 1. Teleport panel

Press **T**, or click "Teleport [T]" at the top left.

- **Search box.** Typing filters the list; **Enter** jumps to the best match. Typing a kind of place lists every place of that kind after the name matches: `dungeon` lists the ten dungeons (the six curated ones and ACE's four indoor places, below), `interior` the two building interiors, `indoors` every indoor place. A kind matches only as a whole word (`dungeon`, `building`, `interior`, `indoors`) or a prefix of at least three letters (`dun`, `int`), so a short query such as `in`, `do` or `or` lists only the places whose name or alias contains it (`TeleportCatalog.MinKindPrefix`; before, `do` appended every "indoors" row).
- **List.** Double-click or Enter on a row jumps there. A row shows the name and, outdoors, retail map coordinates; indoors (retail shows no coordinates there, §2) it says `dungeon` or `building interior` (`indoors` only when the cell could not be read). Its tooltip shows the cell, heading, aliases and data source.
- **Status line.** "Jumped to Town Network (dungeon)."; the kind is left out when the name already says it ("Jumped to Holtburg shop (interior).", "Jumped to Glenden Wood Dungeon."; `TeleportDestination.Label`).
- **Map coordinates.** Type e.g. `42.1N 33.6E` and press Go or Enter (§2).
- **Keyboard.** While a text field has focus the fly camera ignores WASD/QE, because it polls the keyboard. **Esc** gives the focus back.

### Where the destinations come from

The panel's catalogue is `TeleportCatalog.Default()`: ACE's points of interest plus the interiors track's curated dungeons and building interiors (`InteriorLocations`, docs/interiors.md §6), 62 places in all.

**Curated interiors.** ACE's list has no classic dungeon and no building, so the eight `InteriorLocations` entries are added (M1 definition of done item 2 asks for at least three dungeons and a building interior):
- **Dungeons:** Holtburg Redoubt, Mines of Despair, Glenden Wood Dungeon, the Training Academy, the Town Network and the Marketplace.
- **Building interiors:** the Holtburg shop and the Holtburg smithy.
- **Same place, one row.** An interior at an ACE drop point (same cell, origins within 0.5 m) merges into ACE's row, which keeps its name and position and gains the curated name and key as aliases. The Town Network and the Marketplace are such places; the other six get rows of their own, found by name or key (`mines`, `redoubt`, `holtburg-shop`, ...).

**ACE's points of interest.** The client dats do not name places. Lifestone uses ACE's point-of-interest list, the one ACE's `@telepoi` command uses (`HandleTeleportPoi`, Source/ACE.Server/Command/Handlers/AdminCommands.cs, ACE @95ef426d):

- **Chain.** A name in table `points_of_interest` maps to a portal weenie, whose `PositionType.Destination` (2) is the drop point.
- **The extract** is `src/Lifestone.Data/World/Data/ace-world-points-of-interest.json` (AGPL-3.0, embedded in Lifestone.Data).
  - It was generated by `tools/data/extract_ace_poi.py` from the pinned `ACE-World-Database-v0.9.296.sql` (sha256 `7f58832a…a021b4`, from `server/runtime/downloads/ace-world-v0.9.296/`).
  - It reads four tables: `points_of_interest`, `weenie`, `weenie_properties_string` (type 1, Name) and `weenie_properties_position` (type 2, Destination).
- **What it holds:** 62 point-of-interest names for 56 destination weenies.
  - **The display name** comes from the weenie's name: "Portal to Holtburg" becomes Holtburg, "Qalaba'r Portal" becomes Qalaba'r.
  - **Aliases.** The other names ACE accepts are search aliases: Hotel / Swank / HotelSwank / Hotel Swank, TN / TownNetwork, Outpost (Danby's Outpost), Refuge (Oolutanga's Refuge), and so on.
- **What it covers:**
  - **50 outdoor places.** These are every town on the list in the milestone: Holtburg, Arwic, Yaraq, Shoushi, Cragstone, Rithwic, Glenden Wood, Al-Arqas, Samsur, Hebian-To, Sawato, Zaikhal, Tufa, Qalaba'r, Lytelthorpe, Eastham, Baishi, Mayoi, Nanto, Lin, Dryreach, Stonehold, Plateau and Fort Tethana. There are also more towns, outposts and castles (Bandit Castle, Beach Fort, Linvak Tukal, Ayan Baqur, ...).
  - **6 indoor ones:** Underground City, The Marketplace of Dereth, Hotel Swank!, AC Storage, Night Club and Town Network.
  - **Their kind.** The Town Network and the Marketplace take the curated kind (dungeon). The other four are classified when the main scene builds its catalogue (`Navigator.Create` → `TeleportCatalog.ClassifyIndoor`) by their cell's seen_outside flag, the same test the position HUD uses for "indoors (dungeon)" or "indoors (building)" (§4): none of the four is seen_outside, so all four are dungeons (pinned by `CatalogClassificationTests` on the real dats). Before, the panel said "indoors" where the HUD said "indoors (dungeon)", and `dungeon` did not find the Underground City.
- **Pinned by tests:**
  - Holtburg's entry is exactly the core's start location (0xA9B40019 [84 7.1 94], the same as ACViewer's Locations.txt);
  - every outdoor destination's height is the retail ground height within 6 m, which pins the cell/origin reading;
  - all 24 named towns are found by name.

### What a jump does (`Navigator`)

1. **The world recentres.** `WorldRoot.Teleport` drops every landblock and moves the floating origin.
2. **The camera is placed:**
   - **Outdoor, fly mode:** the start view. The camera is 90 m behind the drop point (opposite its facing) and 45 m above it, looking at it, never less than 5 m above the ground (`CameraPlacement.Overview`, as `Main` places the start). The interiors' cell tracker is told the viewer is outdoors.
   - **Outdoor, walk mode:** the eye 1.7 m above the ground on the spot, facing the drop heading.
   - **Indoor** (an EnvCell: a dungeon or a building interior): through the interiors, `InteriorController.PlaceCamera` (docs/interiors.md §6). The feet stand on the destination's floor point and the eye 1.7 m above it, facing its heading; the tracker knows the cell at once (read from the dat), so a dungeon streams indoor from the first frame: its own landblock only, no landscape around it, no outdoor objects ring. Before the fix the panel placed the camera without the cell: the streamer first loaded the neighbouring landblock's landscape (the Town Network's drop point lies at y = -60, in 0x0006) and the first ~0.6 s rendered black.
   - **ACE's re-celling.** ACE moves some dungeon positions into the cell that holds them (`WorldObject.AdjustDungeon`: `AdjustPos` for a few dungeons, then `AdjustDungeonCells`; WorldObject.cs:738). Lifestone uses the raw Destination; when its cell does not hold the point, the cell tracker finds the cell that does once the landblock is in (Hotel Swank!'s drop point names 0x018A024F; the tracker settles in 0x018A0273).
   - **Without interiors** (`--interiors=off`) an indoor jump stands at the cell position and logs a warning that nothing is drawn there.
3. **Streaming** starts from the new spot on the next frame.
4. **The old place** is torn down in the jump frame (every layer detaches at once, so the cell tracker and the objects ring forget it before the camera is placed), but its hidden landblock roots and the nodes still under them (a town's or dungeon's EnvCell meshes) are freed at most 96 nodes a frame, first in each frame's main-thread drain, from the next frame on (`LandblockStreamer.Reset`), not all at the end of the jump frame. A node costs about 25 µs all told (leaving the Training Academy, whose 2,160 cell nodes were all freed at once, the end of the jump frame took 49-67 ms), so this bounds the cost at about 2-3 ms a frame; it is counted in nodes because the rendering server's share of a free falls outside the main-thread budget. The streamer is not idle until they are gone.

### Measuring jumps (`--teleport-tour`)

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--teleport-tour[=LAPS]` | Jump through the stops LAPS times (default 1), measuring each jump, then quit (an automated run) |
| `--teleport-stops=NAME[,NAME]` | The stops (names or keys; default ten towns and indoor places, below) |

`--teleport-tour[=LAPS]` jumps through teleport destinations the way the panel does (`Navigator.Teleport`), waiting before each jump until the current place is complete plus 30 frames, and logs per jump: the synchronous call, the jump frame (from its start to the next frame's start, split into the process phase and the rest: end-of-frame frees and render), the slowest frame in the second after it (and the garbage-collection pause inside that frame), and the time until the new place is complete (`CaptureReadiness`: streamer idle, objects ring idle, every expected EnvCell attached, main-thread queue empty). `--teleport-stops=a,b,...` picks the stops (names or keys; default: Holtburg, Arwic, Yaraq, Town Network, Shoushi, Underground City, Cragstone, Marketplace, Glenden Wood, Rithwic). It quits with 0 when every stop was found and completed.

```
pwsh -File tools\godot.ps1 -Run -Offscreen -IsolateUserData -TimeoutSeconds 400 -Resolution 1280x720 -GameArgs:'--teleport-tour=3'
```

Measured on the RTX 5090 / Ryzen 9950X3D machine, Debug build, 30 workers, nothing else running, default stops. "Roots freed at once" is this fix with the original `Reset` (every old root queue-freed in the jump frame); "old roots deferred" freed them from the next frame on in budgeted steps, as the final code does (the final code caps the frees at 96 nodes a frame instead of by time; the jump frame runs the same code in both):

|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| master @110ad6e | on | 20 | 8.0 / 19.9 ms | 32.3 / 48.7 ms | 12.8 / 28.3 ms | 0.15 / 0.91 s |
| this fix, roots freed at once | on | 30 | 9.7 / 34.8 ms | 31.8 / 58.0 ms | 17.4 / 52.7 ms | 0.19 / 1.21 s |
| this fix, old roots deferred | on | 30 | 8.5 / 25.6 ms | **22.3 / 41.6 ms** | 18.6 / 33.1 ms | 0.18 / 0.90 s |
| this fix, roots freed at once | off | 30 | 9.3 / 26.2 ms | 31.5 / 48.1 ms | 19.0 / 46.5 ms | 0.18 / 0.99 s |
| this fix, old roots deferred | off | 30 | 9.0 / 24.3 ms | **24.4 / 46.7 ms** | 18.8 / 31.7 ms | 0.18 / 1.07 s |

- **The jump frame** drops by about a quarter to a third (median 32 to 22-24 ms) and its worst case by 1-16 ms; its end (frees and render) averaged 23.3 ms with the roots freed at once and 14.3 ms deferred. "The slowest frame in the next second" is the new place's attach work and GC; it varies run to run (master's three 2-lap runs gave medians of 12.2, 12.8 and 18.0 ms) and is unchanged within that noise.
- **Under load** (two or three other Godot instances running, interleaved runs of the original `Reset` and the final 96-node cap, 2 laps each, vsync on) the difference shrinks into the noise: jump frame medians 28.3-34.7 ms (original) against 28.5-33.2 ms (final), end of the jump frame 21.7-26.0 against 19.9-23.6 ms; the next second's slowest frame without its GC pause 5.6-7.8 against 5.5-7.5 ms. Frames of 70-200 ms with no GC and no Lifestone work in them appear in both under load (other processes' start-ups), as the perf audit found.
- **Dungeons and building interiors** (stops Holtburg, Mines of Despair, Arwic, Training Academy, Holtburg shop, Glenden Wood Dungeon, Yaraq, Holtburg Redoubt): indoor jumps complete in 0.06-0.36 s (the Training Academy's 2,163 cells 0.75 s the first time); leaving the Training Academy for the shop cost a 49-71 ms jump frame with its roots freed at once and 40-44 ms deferred.
- **What is left in the jump frame** is the layers' own detach (measured per layer for 289 landblocks: objects 2.5-8 ms, terrain about 2 ms, interiors about 1 ms, all in the call) and the end-of-frame frees of the nodes the terrain and objects layers queue for deletion themselves (289 terrain meshes, the objects ring's batches). Spreading those would be a change in those layers (open point).
- **Integrated build (M1 integration, all wave-3 fixes merged, 8 workers, server GC, idle machine, vsync on, `--teleport-tour=2`, three runs of 20 jumps):** call median 9.1-9.9 ms (max 18-28 ms); jump frame median **21.4-23.8 ms** (max 34-41 ms); slowest frame in the next second median 10.0-12.5 ms (max 15-29 ms; without its GC pause 5.7-7.6 ms); complete after median 0.18-0.20 s (max 0.72 s); 0 failures. That matches the deferred row above. The master-against-branch A/B was not repeated: the tour harness only exists on this branch, and the one-off build with roots freed at once (the second row) is that comparison. The deferred frees merged cleanly with the perf track's changes (`LandblockStreamer.cs` was not touched by it).
- **The audit's 135-245 ms** were not the teleport: its harness saved a PNG in the frame before each jump, and both the overlay's 240-frame maximum and the sky's slow-frame log (`[sky] slow frame ms=134` logged right after the jump) counted that frame. The jump frame itself was the ~40 ms frame after it.

## 2. Map coordinates

Retail shows the coordinates of the player's **24 m outdoor cell**, not of the exact position (`CPlayerSystem::InqPlayerCoords`, acclient.c:398264: `(lcoord - 1024) x 0.1 + 0.5`; `AcCoordinates`). So each 0.1 step names one cell.

`MapCoordinateParser` inverts this with `lcoord = round((value - 0.5) / 0.1) + 1024`, and the camera goes to the centre of that cell.

| Input | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `42.1N 33.6E`, `42.1N, 33.6E`, `33.6E 42.1N`, `42.1 n 33.6 e` | Holtburg's drop cell 0xA9B40019 |
| `42.1, -33.6` | signed, latitude first like retail's text; negative = S/W |
| `42.14N 33.64E` | extra decimals are rounded to retail's 0.1 grid (half away from zero) |
| `150N 33E`, `42.1N`, `42.1N 12S`, `1 2 3` | refused with a message (off the map, missing axis, two latitudes, three numbers) |

The map spans 101.9S to 102.0N and 101.9W to 102.0E. A test round-trips every displayed coordinate on a 7 x 11 grid of cells through retail's text and back.

## 3. Golden viewpoints

A fixed list of views rendered to PNGs with a manifest, so the owner can stand in the same place at the same Dereth time in retail and compare (M1 definition of done item 11).

```
pwsh -File tools\godot.ps1 -Run -Offscreen -IsolateUserData -TimeoutSeconds 600 -Resolution 1280x720 `
  -GameArgs:'--golden-viewpoints'
```

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--golden-viewpoints[=DIR]` | Output folder, default `docs/screenshots/m1/golden` (gitignored). A relative folder is relative to the repository |
| `--golden-only=ID[,ID]` | Only these views: an id or a unique fragment, e.g. `01,yaraq` |
| `--golden-frames=N` | Frames rendered after the place is complete and the sky is built, before each capture (default 45); the capture also waits at least 2 s, so the statics' flames and smoke have reached their steady state |

**What M1 shows.** The views draw what the dats hold: terrain, scenery, LandBlockInfo statics and buildings, EnvCells and their statics, and the sky. Server-spawned objects (lifestones, portals, doors, signs, wells, NPCs) come from ACE's `CreateObject` in M2, so they are missing here but present in retail. That is not a regression; the manifest says so too.

Buildings and statics come from the objects layer; the old `--smoke-statics` flag is retired (M1 wave 3): it is reported as a bad argument and ignored.

### Per view

1. The clock stops at the view's game time.
2. The camera jumps there. Indoor views (the target is an EnvCell) go through the interiors exactly as a teleport-panel jump does (`Navigator.PlaceIndoors`: on the floor point, eyes 1.7 m up, the cell known at once), then turn to the view's heading and pitch.
3. Every HUD and panel is hidden: each `CanvasLayer` in the scene (the core HUD, the interiors' and objects' status lines, the tool panels, the About panel; `NavigationUi.SetHudVisible`).
4. The runner waits until the place is complete (`CaptureReadiness`: streamer idle, objects ring idle, every expected EnvCell attached, main-thread queue empty) and every sky model is built, then renders N frames, and at least 2 s (`CaptureReadiness.SettleSeconds`: the statics' default scripts start when their landblock is created, and flames, smoke and fountains need a second or two to reach a steady state), and saves `<id>.png`. The same readiness check and the same settle gate `--screenshot` (so `--start=mines-of-despair --screenshot=... --frames=1` is never black, and its torches burn), and the frame that found the place complete is drawn before the capture.

At the end it writes `manifest.json` and `manifest.md`, then quits with 0 when every view was captured.

**The time.** Each view names a day fraction and a day group (weather). The runner takes the first day from Morningthaw 1, 10 P.Y. whose retail day group is that group (`SkyDesc::CalcPresentDayGroup`). So the same date shows the same sky in retail, with no forcing.

### The manifest, per view

- **Position:**
  - the outdoor cell and its local x, y, z; indoors the EnvCell and the eye relative to that cell's own landblock (dungeon cells often lie outside its 192 m square);
  - global metres;
  - retail map coordinates (none indoors: retail shows none there);
  - ACE's admin command to stand there, e.g. `@teleloc 0xA9B30015 [69.921 110.208 139.000] 0.996917 0 0 -0.078459` (cell, origin, then the quaternion w x y z of the heading). Indoors it names the floor point, e.g. `@teleloc 0x01880307 [30.000 -70.000 0.000] 1.000000 0 0 0.000000`.
- **Camera:** heading, pitch and vertical field of view (60° in explore mode and the video shots, which set their own; play mode draws retail's 73° at 800x600, docs/game-core.md `--fov`).
- **Time:** the game time, the retail date and time strings, the day fraction and the day group.
- **Light and fog:** the sun elevation, heading, colour and brightness; the ambient colour and level; the fog range and colour. For a view in a dungeon cell (not seen_outside) the table says "indoor: ambient 0.2, static lights; time of day does not apply" instead (retail lights such cells with 0.2 white ambient and their burned-in static lights, `CellManager::ChangePosition`, docs/interiors.md); `manifest.json` still records the sky's values, as `indoorLighting` beside them. Building interiors keep the outdoor values (seen_outside: the sky lights them).
- **The resolution.**

### Matching the time in retail

Retail takes game time from the server, so a stock client cannot set it.

- **ACE** starts its clock from real time (`DerethDateTime.UtcNowToEMUTime`). A matching server clock or retail's local `GameTime.TimeZeroDelta` offset (`GameSky::OnTimeAdjustment`) gives the date.
- **The sky depends only on the time of day and the day group.** Any date whose day group matches, at the same fraction, shows the same sky.
- **The camera** in the views is a flying camera. Use the heading and pitch, and stand as close to the position as the character allows.

## 4. Camera and position HUD

- **Fly mode keeps above the ground outdoors** (`FlyGroundClearance`, used by `FlyCamera`; the cell comes from the interiors' cell tracker through `NavigationUi`). Terrain is drawn back-face culled, as retail draws it, so a camera under it outdoors would see the sky through the ground. Retail has no free camera and its player never goes under the terrain, so this is the M1 stand-in, not retail behaviour. Per fly step:
  - A step that ends at or above ground + 0.5 m (`FlyCamera.FlyClearance`, exact retail `TerrainSampler` heights), or that stays in the cells (the viewer's cell, its portal neighbours or its stab list, the tracker's own first tests), is never clamped.
  - **Dungeons stay reachable.** From a cell that is not seen_outside (retail's flag; every cell of a dungeon landblock), the free camera may leave the cells through a wall or a floor, which retail's physics never allows; it is not pushed up out of the ground then, and moves freely, under or over the terrain, until it is back in the open air (at or above ground + 0.5 m) more than 20 m from that landblock's cells. So it can fly back in, or on down to a lower level through the space between cells (the Mines of Despair's terrain, at z 0, lies between its levels). Out there it sees the sky through the culled terrain, as it did before the clamp existed; retail never shows that place.
  - **Cellars too.** A town's cells are all seen_outside (all 138 of Holtburg's 0xA9B4, the 15 outside its buildings' stab lists too), so a cellar is told by its height: a cell whose top lies below ground + 0.5 m at the eye (Holtburg has such rooms, e.g. 0xA9B40180, z 71.7-77.2 under ground at 79.07). Leaving one through a wall keeps the eye under the ground until it comes up into the open air.
  - **Building interiors** (the other seen_outside cells; their floors lie on the terrain, the Holtburg shop's at z 94.00 on ground at 94.00): a step that leaves the cells below ground + 0.5 m goes no lower than the eye already was, so Q stops on the floor instead of sinking into the ground.
  - **Everything else outdoors** ending below ground + 0.5 m is lifted to it: open air, and an explicit placement under the ground (a teleport, `--interior-eye` on an outdoor cell), lifted on the next step.
  - **Measured** (scratch harness holding keys for 1-2 s at 40 m/s and logging the HUD every frame; review run at a369e93 = before, this fix = after):

- **Position HUD indoors.** In an EnvCell the HUD shows that cell's own landblock and cell, "indoors (dungeon)" or "indoors (building)", and the eye relative to the cell's landblock; no map coordinates. Retail shows none there: `CPlayerSystem::InqPlayerCoords` (acclient.c:398264) succeeds only when `LandDefs::gid_to_lcoord` (209521) does, and that refuses cell indices of 0x100 and above. Before the fix the HUD converted the global position as if outdoors and named the neighbouring landblock (Town Network: "0x0006 ... 96.6S, 101.7W").
