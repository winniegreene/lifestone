# Milestone 1: explore Dereth

**Goal:** fly and walk around the whole of Dereth in Lifestone. Everything comes from the user's own dats, with no server and no networking yet:
- textured terrain with the retail blending, and roads;
- procedural scenery;
- static objects and buildings;
- building interiors and dungeons;
- sky, sun, lighting and fog with time of day;
- smooth streaming.

M1 is the visual foundation. M2 (login to the local ACE, movement) will build on it.

## Definition of done
1. **Launch.** `tools\godot.ps1 -Run` opens at Holtburg.
2. **Camera.**
   - Free-fly: WASD plus mouse look, Shift for fast, wheel for speed.
   - Walk: follows the terrain height; no collision yet.
   - A teleport panel with the major towns plus at least 3 dungeons and a building interior.
3. **Terrain** uses the retail TexMerge blend (palCode, overlays with alpha maps and rotations, roads, TexTiling) and retail per-vertex lighting. No visible seams between landblocks.
4. **Scenery** (trees, rocks, plants) is generated by the retail algorithm. The 0xA9B3 conformance test passes.
5. **Statics and buildings** from LandBlockInfo render with correct textures and translucency, alpha-tested clip maps, luminous surfaces and LOD (Degrade).
6. **EnvCells** (interiors and dungeons) render with the correct surfaces. Visibility uses the retail portal and VisibleCells data, not "draw everything".
7. **Sky**: sky objects, sun direction, ambient and directional light colours, and fog from the Region SkyDesc keyframes, driven by Dereth game time. There is a time-of-day slider.
8. **Streaming.**
   - Landblock rings load and unload around the camera on worker threads, with a per-frame main-thread budget.
   - A floating origin rebases at landblock granularity.
   - Flying Holtburg → Arwic at fast speed never hitches more than 50 ms.
9. **Performance:** at least 60 fps in Holtburg and in a dense dungeon on the development machine. Draw calls and memory are reported.
10. **Tests.**
    - Every decoder runs over every relevant record in the real dats (byte-exact where the format allows).
    - Algorithm conformance tests use reference implementations ported from ACE or the retail decompile.
    - `dotnet test` is green.
11. **Golden viewpoints:** a harness renders a fixed list of viewpoints to `docs/screenshots/m1/` (gitignored) for review against the retail client.

**Scope notes (lead decisions, wave 3; see also the decisions after Status):**
- **Server-spawned objects are M2.** Lifestones, portals, doors, signs, wells, statues, forges, chests and NPCs are ACE weenies the server creates with `CreateObject`; they arrive with M2's login to the local ACE. M1 draws only what the dats hold (LandBlockInfo and EnvCell statics, buildings, scenery), so retail comparisons of towns will show them missing; that is expected, not a regression (docs/objects.md, header).
- **Statics are alive.** Statics, buildings and EnvCell statics run their Setup's default script and animation as retail does (torch flames, chimney smoke, braziers, fountains, birds, flags): docs/objects.md §1.7.

## Lead decisions (M1 wave 3)
- **Fidelity policy for rendering artifacts.** Lifestone reproduces retail's art and behaviour, but not rendering
  artifacts retail players would call glitches. The fix is the documented default and a flag restores exact retail
  (fidelity rules below).
- **Seamless terrain normals and the follow sky anchor are the defaults.** `--retail-normals` restores the light step at
  every landblock edge; `--retail-sky-anchor` (or `--sky-anchor=retail`) restores the sky's 192 m jump at every
  landblock crossing.
- **Byte-space blending is the default.** It is retail's arithmetic (D3D9 blended raw bytes); `--blend-space=linear`
  gives Godot's linear-light blending. Godot's built-in materials hand their colours over through
  `ByteSpaceBlending.BuiltinColor` (render-contract.md §2.3; a source test enforces it).
- **Server-spawned objects arrive in M2.** Lifestones, portals, doors, signs, wells, statues, forges, chests and NPCs
  are ACE weenies created with `CreateObject`. M1 draws only what the dats hold, so retail town comparisons show them
  missing; that is expected, not a regression.
- **Debug assemblies are acceptable until packaging.** Every M1 measurement is a Debug build launched through
  `tools\godot.ps1`. Release exports, and server GC in them, come with packaging.
- **DoD 8 is judged on the raw maximum frame**, as worded. On a shared machine `tools\smoke.ps1` retries the flight
  once, only when every frame over 50 ms had no Lifestone work in it.
- **Frame pacing and workers.** Vsync stays on by default; vsync off or mailbox is capped at the screen's refresh rate,
  and uncapped measurements add `--max-fps=0`. Workers default to min(8, cores − 2). Load radius 8 and the 4 ms budget
  stay.

## Architecture rules
- **Lifestone.Data** (net10.0, no Godot) owns all decoding and all algorithms. It outputs plain data in AC coordinates (Z up, CCW front faces): see `src/Lifestone.Data/Geometry/MeshData.cs`.
  - All dat reads go through `DatSet.TryGet*` or `DatSet.Read`, which hold the lock.
- **Lifestone.Client** (net10.0, no Godot, no I/O) owns the engine-agnostic client logic: the streaming plan, worker pool and frame budget, resource caches, the option registry, objects and LOD, living statics, interior visibility, the sky director and navigation (game-core.md §1). It is tested in `tests/Lifestone.Client.Tests/`.
- **Lifestone.Game** (Godot) is presentation only.
  - The AC→Godot axis and winding conversion happens only in `scripts/Core/AcToGodot.cs`.
  - Godot resources are created on the main thread, within a per-frame budget. The one allowance: workers build CPU-side Godot `Image`s while decoding textures (render-contract.md §0); uploads, meshes, materials and nodes stay on the main thread.
  - Every command-line option is registered, with its help, in `src/Lifestone.Client/Options/OptionCatalog.cs` (`OptionCatalogTests` fails otherwise); a check suite is one per run in Main's check mode.
- **Caching.** Derived data may go to `<repo>\cache\` (gitignored), keyed by `DatSet.Iterations.CacheKey` plus a decoder version. M1 needed no persistent cache (the key is only logged), and nothing derived from the dats is ever committed.
- **No new NuGet packages** without lead approval.

## Fidelity rules (lessons from an earlier prototype)
- **Cite every algorithm and constant** in a code comment, using a pinned source:
  - ACE `vendor-eval/ACE` @ v1.78.4816 (95ef426d), AGPL-3.0;
  - OpenAC `vendor-eval/OpenAC` @ 1ee5a08, MIT. Mind the deviations listed in `docs/research/foundation-openac.md`;
  - ACViewer (GPL-3.0, combinable with our AGPL-3.0);
  - WorldBuilder (MIT per its README);
  - the retail decompile (Mag-nus/acclient-AI-RE: 11.4186 with PDB names, 11.6096 matching the user's exe), by function name.
- **Never copy decompiled Turbine code into the repo.** Re-express its behaviour and cite the function.
- **Ported files** carry a header naming the source repo, SHA, path and licence. Each track lists its sources in `docs/third-party/<track>.md`.
- **No invented behaviour.** If something is unknown, make it a named, logged TODO that fails loudly. Do not guess silently.
- **Tests use the real dats**, via the `RequiresDats` attributes. Synthetic-only tests do not count as conformance.
- **Glitch fixes (lead policy, wave 3).** Reproduce retail's art and behaviour, but not rendering artifacts retail players would call glitches. Such a fix is the documented default, and a flag restores exact retail. So far:
  - seamless terrain normals (`--retail-normals` restores the landblock-edge light step; terrain-renderer.md §8);
  - a sky that follows the camera's x and y (`--retail-sky-anchor` restores the 192 m jump at every landblock edge; sky.md §4.2).

  Retail's byte-space blending (`--blend-space=linear` for Godot's) and detail textures (`--detail=off`) are retail behaviour, not deviations (render-contract.md §2.3–2.4).
